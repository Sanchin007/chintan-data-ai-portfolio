01 / Project overview

From fragmented procurement work to guided, reviewable decisions

The agent supported a broad procurement workflow rather than a single conversational use case. It helped interpret requests, work across procurement information, identify exceptions and route issues to the right human reviewers. People remained responsible for verification, approvals and consequential decisions.

What the agent supported

A connected set of procurement activities, spanning the information needed to evaluate suppliers, compare commercial options, review purchasing documentation and surface potential risks.

  • Supplier onboarding, qualification and evaluation support.
  • Requisition, sourcing, quotation comparison and purchase-order workflows.
  • Invoice and supporting-document review, where relevant to the process.
  • Commercial analysis, spend visibility and supplier-related exceptions.

How decisions were governed

The agent was used to accelerate analysis and highlight matters needing attention—not to replace procurement, legal or compliance accountability.

  • Human guidance shaped the task and its context.
  • Potential inconsistencies and risk signals were surfaced for review.
  • Export and trade-law concerns were escalated to a compliance specialist.
  • Verification and final decisions remained with authorised people.
02 / Procurement workflow

A broad workflow, coordinated around human checkpoints

The following view groups the confirmed breadth of the procurement use case into functional stages. It is a process-level representation, not a claim that every organisation uses the same sequence or that every stage runs without human input.

01 / INTAKE

Request understanding

Interpret the procurement need, clarify context and identify the information or documents required to proceed.

02 / SUPPLIERS

Supplier intelligence

Support supplier onboarding and evaluation by organising available information and surfacing gaps or inconsistencies.

03 / SOURCING

Commercial comparison

Assist with quotation comparison, sourcing analysis and review of commercial terms against the stated requirement.

04 / PURCHASING

Requisition to order

Support requisition and purchase-order workflows, with people retaining the relevant approval authority.

05 / DOCUMENTS

Document intelligence

Review procurement-related documents and bring relevant details, missing information and potential mismatches to attention.

06 / CONTROL

Exceptions and escalation

Flag potential policy, trade or export-compliance issues and route cases to the appropriate specialist for verification.

04 / Agent operating cycle

How a procurement issue moves through the process

A representative sequence showing the decision-support pattern: the agent works on the task, exposes what needs attention, and hands consequential questions to a human owner.

STEP 01

STEP 02

STEP 03

05 / Export compliance & trade law

Risk flagging with specialist escalation—not automated legal clearance

Export compliance and trade-law frameworks formed part of the control context. The agent's role was to flag potential issues and push them to a compliance specialist to verify. This distinction is central to how the workflow should be represented.

AI-ASSISTED IDENTIFICATION

Potential issue detection

Bring possible export-control, trade-restriction, sanctions-related or documentation concerns to attention when relevant information indicates that a specialist review may be needed.

SPECIALIST REVIEW

Escalation and verification

Send flagged issues to a compliance specialist, who checks the underlying evidence, applicable jurisdiction and current requirements before deciding what action is appropriate.

Framework-aware review context

Relevant frameworks may include export-control rules, dual-use controls, sanctions and restricted-party requirements, customs and trade documentation obligations, and applicable national or regional trade laws.

Evidence before conclusion

Potential flags are review prompts, not findings of a confirmed violation. The human reviewer must validate identity, goods classification, destination, end-user, end-use, licensing position and supporting documents as applicable.

Important control boundary: The agent flagged potential issues and escalated them. It did not independently issue export licences, make binding legal determinations, or replace an authorised compliance specialist's verification and decision.
06 / Human-in-the-loop governance

Automation for analysis. People for accountability.

Human guidance and verification were part of the operating model, not an afterthought. The agent helped move work forward while keeping consequential decisions with the responsible business or specialist owner.

Human oversight at the point of consequence

The system surfaced information and potential risks. People supplied judgement, verified the evidence and retained decision authority.

GuidanceHuman-defined task, context and intended outcome.
VerificationReview of source evidence and potential false positives.
EscalationSpecialist involvement for trade and export issues.
ApprovalAuthorised human decision before consequential action.

AI agent responsibilities

  • Interpret procurement requests and organise relevant context.
  • Assist with information review and commercial comparison.
  • Identify gaps, mismatches and potential risk indicators.
  • Flag and route matters requiring specialist attention.

Human responsibilities

  • Provide guidance and confirm business context.
  • Validate source documents and the agent's observations.
  • Assess compliance issues against applicable requirements.
  • Make or approve final procurement and compliance decisions.
07 / Delivery contribution

Enterprise AI applied to a control-sensitive business process

The value of the approach was not simply generating answers. It was helping procurement teams work through complex information while making exceptions visible and ensuring specialist review remained part of the process.

Broader workflow coverage

One agent experience supporting multiple procurement activities rather than a narrowly scoped FAQ interface.

Earlier visibility of exceptions

Potential issues could be surfaced for attention instead of being buried in fragmented records and documents.

Clearer accountability

Human guidance, compliance-specialist verification and authorised decision-making remained explicit control points.

No quantitative impact, processing-volume, accuracy or savings figures are stated here because project-specific measurements have not yet been confirmed for this case study.
